Since POPIA's compliance deadline of 1 July 2021, the Protection of Personal Information Act has applied to virtually every South African business. Yet many owners still aren't sure what "being compliant" actually looks like in practice. The good news: compliance is a series of concrete, achievable steps - not a mysterious black box.

Here's the ten-step checklist we use with clients, in the order we recommend tackling it.

## 1. Secure leadership buy-in and accountability

POPIA's very first condition is **accountability**. Compliance can't live in a drawer - someone at the top needs to own it. Get leadership to formally acknowledge POPIA as a priority and allocate a modest budget and time. This single step unblocks everything that follows.

## 2. Map your personal information (build a data inventory)

You can't protect what you don't know you have. Document what personal information you collect, where it comes from, why you hold it, where it's stored, who can access it and who you share it with. A simple spreadsheet is a perfectly good start.

> **Tip:** Walk through a typical customer and employee "journey" - from first contact to offboarding. It surfaces data flows you'd otherwise miss, like backups, spreadsheets and third-party tools.

## 3. Appoint and register your Information Officer

By default your Information Officer is the head of the organisation (the CEO, MD or owner). You can delegate duties to Deputy Information Officers, but the role must be **registered with the Information Regulator** before performing its functions. Registration is free and done through the Regulator's portal.

## 4. Establish a lawful basis for processing

For each processing activity, confirm you have a lawful basis - consent, a contract, a legal obligation, a legitimate interest, or protection of the data subject. Don't default to consent for everything; often another basis is more appropriate and more robust.

## 5. Publish clear privacy notices

POPIA's **openness** condition means people must know what you do with their information. Publish a plain-language privacy notice on your website and at the points where you collect data, explaining what you collect, why, who you share it with and how people can exercise their rights.

## 6. Put operator (processor) agreements in place

Any third party that processes personal information on your behalf - payroll, cloud hosting, a marketing agency - is an "operator". POPIA requires a **written contract** obliging them to keep the information secure and process it only on your instructions.

## 7. Implement security safeguards

Apply reasonable technical and organisational measures: access controls, strong passwords and multi-factor authentication, encryption where appropriate, regular backups and a patching routine. Document what you've done - "reasonable" is judged partly on whether you can show your reasoning.

## 8. Create a data-subject request process

People have the right to access, correct and delete their information and to object to processing. Decide in advance who handles these requests, how you'll verify identity and how you'll respond within a reasonable time. A simple internal procedure avoids panic later.

## 9. Prepare a breach (security compromise) response plan

If personal information is accessed by an unauthorised person, POPIA requires you to notify the Information Regulator and affected people as soon as reasonably possible. Have a written plan - who to call, how to contain it, what to record and how to notify - ready *before* anything happens.

## 10. Train your staff - and keep it fresh

Your people are the front line. A short, practical awareness session dramatically reduces day-to-day risk (think misdirected emails, weak passwords and oversharing). Refresh training annually and build it into onboarding.

> Compliance isn't a once-off project - it's a habit. The businesses that stay compliant are the ones that review, refresh and keep their data inventory alive.
