Do You Really Need an Information Officer? POPIA Explained

Short answer: yes — and you almost certainly already have one, whether you've thought about it or not. Here's what that means and what you need to do.

Who is your Information Officer?

Under POPIA, the Information Officer is automatically the head of your organisation — the CEO, managing director, or in a small business, the owner. You don't "hire" one; the role attaches to whoever leads the business, unless it is formally delegated in writing.

This surprises many owners. There's no need to create a new job title or bring in an external expert to be your Information Officer. But the person holding the role does carry real responsibilities.

What does the Information Officer have to do?

The Information Officer is responsible for ensuring the business complies with POPIA. In practice, their duties include:

  • Encouraging and monitoring the organisation's compliance with POPIA;
  • Dealing with requests made to the organisation (including access requests under PAIA);
  • Working with the Information Regulator on investigations;
  • Ensuring a compliance framework is developed, implemented and maintained;
  • Making sure a personal information impact assessment is done where needed; and
  • Ensuring internal measures and adequate awareness are in place.

Good to know

The head of the organisation remains ultimately accountable even after delegating duties. Delegation shares the workload — it doesn't transfer the buck entirely.

What about Deputy Information Officers?

Because the head of a business can't personally do everything, POPIA allows the appointment of Deputy Information Officers to carry out the day-to-day functions. Larger organisations often designate deputies per division or region. Deputies should also be registered with the Regulator.

Do you have to register?

Yes. Information Officers (and their deputies) must be registered with the Information Regulator before they perform their functions. Registration is free and is done via the Regulator's online portal. You'll provide your organisation's details and the designated officer's details.

Registering is quick and free — but skipping it is exactly the kind of easily-avoided gap that undermines an otherwise solid compliance effort.

Getting it right

Appointing and registering your Information Officer is one of the earliest steps on the road to compliance — but it works best alongside the governance, policies and training that give the role something to stand on.
