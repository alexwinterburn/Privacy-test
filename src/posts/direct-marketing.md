## The default rule: opt-in consent

POPIA treats direct marketing by electronic means — email, SMS and automated calls — with particular care. For people who are **not already your customers**, the default is that you need their **opt-in consent** before you can market to them. And you may generally approach a person only *once* to request that consent.

In other words, you can't buy a list and start emailing it. That single misunderstanding is behind a large share of the marketing complaints the Information Regulator receives.

## The existing-customer exception

There's an important, practical exception. You may market **similar products or services to your own existing customers** without fresh opt-in consent, provided:

- you obtained their contact details in the context of a sale;
- you're marketing your own similar products or services; and
- you gave them a reasonable opportunity to opt out — free of charge — both when you collected their details and in every message since.

> **In practice:** This is why a genuine, working "unsubscribe" link matters so much. It's not just courtesy — for existing-customer marketing, that easy opt-out is part of what makes the messaging lawful.

## Every message needs the basics

Whatever the basis, each direct-marketing communication must:

- identify the sender (no hiding who you are);
- contain the sender's contact details; and
- offer a simple, free way to opt out — which you then honour promptly.

> Consent you can prove is worth far more than consent you assume. Keep records of when and how each contact opted in.

## A quick compliance sanity-check

Before your next campaign, ask:

- Is each recipient either an existing customer (similar products) or someone who opted in?
- Can I show *when* and *how* they consented or became a customer?
- Does every message identify us and include a working opt-out?
- Do we action opt-outs quickly and suppress those contacts going forward?

If any answer is "not sure", that's a gap worth closing before it becomes a complaint.
